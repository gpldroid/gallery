The station clock had stopped at 11:47, though the trains, stubbornly, continued to arrive.

Mara stood beneath it with a suitcase that had lost one of its wheels and a paper cup of coffee growing cold between her hands. She had not planned to be here. In fact, she had spent the better part of the evening planning not to be anywhere at all.

The platform smelled of rain and old newspapers. Somewhere beyond the glass doors, a vending machine hummed to itself. A man in a blue coat read the same page of a newspaper for the third time.

When the train arrived, it did so without ceremony. Its windows were warm squares of light against the dark.

Mara chose a seat beside the window. Across from her, a woman with silver hair was knitting something small and green.

“Going far?” the woman asked.

Mara looked at the black countryside slipping into view. “I haven't decided.”

The woman smiled as though this were a perfectly reasonable destination.

For a while, they travelled without speaking. The landscape opened and closed around them: a lit farmhouse, a river holding the moon, the dark outline of trees. Mara watched her reflection hover over the passing world and tried, for once, not to look away.

“I used to take this train every Sunday,” the woman said. “Before I knew where I was going.”

“Did you find out?”

“Eventually. But that was never the useful part.”

Mara turned from the window.

The woman held up the little green thing in her hands. It was becoming a bird, or perhaps a leaf. It was difficult to tell, and perhaps that was the point.

“You can get off wherever you like,” she said. “That's the lovely thing about a train.”

At the next station, Mara stepped onto the platform. The air was cold and clean. There was a bakery across the road, its lights already on, and a narrow street leading towards the river.

She stood there for a moment with her suitcase and her unfinished plans.

Then she began to walk.

The train pulled away behind her, carrying the woman in the blue-lit carriage towards a place neither of them needed to name.

For the first time in weeks, Mara was not late for anything.
