On Tuesday, the light fell differently across the kitchen floor.

It found the old wooden table, crossed the chipped blue bowl, and rested for a moment on the folded newspaper. Nothing remarkable happened. The kettle clicked off. A neighbour closed a door. Somewhere, a dog barked at a delivery van.

And yet, for the first time in a long while, I stopped.

I had been moving through my days as if they were corridors: one thing leading to the next, each door opened only long enough to pass through. I knew the shape of my mornings without seeing them. I could make coffee while thinking about tomorrow.

But the light was there, asking for nothing.

So I watched it.

I noticed the small crack in the bowl, the dust turning slowly in the air, the plant on the windowsill leaning towards a brightness it could not understand. I noticed that the room was quiet, and that quiet was not the same as emptiness.

Perhaps attention is a kind of gratitude. Not the grand, ceremonial kind, but something smaller. A way of saying: I was here. I saw this. It mattered enough to keep.

The light moved on.

I made another cup of tea, and the day continued. But it no longer felt like a corridor. It felt, briefly, like a room I was allowed to inhabit.
