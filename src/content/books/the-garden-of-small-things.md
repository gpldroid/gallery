Some books ask us to travel far. This one asks us to stay where we are, and look again.

Across a series of brief, thoughtful essays, Elias Rowan writes about the ordinary rituals that quietly shape a life: opening a window, walking the same street, remembering a voice, watching the afternoon change.

The Garden of Small Things is an invitation to slow down. Its pages do not offer instructions so much as companionship, reminding us that wonder is rarely far away. Often, it is waiting in the details we have learned to overlook.

**A note from the reading room**

This is a book to keep beside your favourite chair. Read a chapter in the morning, return to a paragraph at dusk, or open it anywhere when the day feels too hurried.
